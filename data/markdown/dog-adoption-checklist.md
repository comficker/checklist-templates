# Dog Adoption Checklist

A dog adoption checklist guiding new owners through adopting a dog, from shelter visit to the first days home.

> Free printable & editable version: [Dog Adoption Checklist on ChecklistForge](https://checklistforge.com/checklist/dog-adoption-checklist) — tick items online or download as PDF.

## Before you adopt a dog

- [ ] Assess your space, yard, and time for walks and training.
- [ ] Decide on a size, age, and activity level that fits your life.
- [ ] Confirm housing rules, breed restrictions, and deposits if you rent.
- [ ] Budget for food, vet care, grooming, and supplies.
- [ ] Discuss responsibilities and a daily routine with your household.
- [ ] Consider how a dog will fit with children or existing pets.

## Visiting the shelter or rescue

- [ ] Research reputable shelters, humane societies, and breed rescues.
- [ ] Confirm hours and whether you need an appointment. — _Call ahead to be sure a dog is still available._
- [ ] Observe how dogs respond to people and to the kennel environment.
- [ ] Ask staff about each dog's history, health, and temperament.
- [ ] Note which dogs match your criteria for a follow-up meet.
- [ ] Be patient if the right dog is not there on your first visit.

## Meeting and matching

- [ ] Spend unhurried one-on-one time with a dog before deciding.
- [ ] Watch body language for comfort, fear, or stress signals.
- [ ] Take the dog for a short walk if the shelter allows it.
- [ ] Introduce household members and existing dogs in a controlled meet.
- [ ] Ask about energy, training, and any behavior challenges.
- [ ] Choose based on fit, not only appearance.

## Adoption paperwork and fees

- [ ] Confirm the adoption fee and what it includes.
- [ ] Collect vaccination, spay or neuter, and microchip records.
- [ ] Sign the adoption contract and keep your copy.
- [ ] Update the microchip registration to your details.
- [ ] Get the dog's current food and feeding schedule.
- [ ] Ask about any recommended follow-up vet care.

## Preparing your home for a dog

- [ ] Dog-proof rooms by removing hazards and securing trash.
- [ ] Set up a crate or quiet space where the dog can decompress.
- [ ] Gather a collar, ID tag, leash, harness, bed, and bowls.
- [ ] Stock the food the dog is already eating to avoid stomach upset.
- [ ] Check that the yard and fences are secure with no gaps.
- [ ] Schedule a first wellness visit with your veterinarian.

## First days home

- [ ] Keep the homecoming calm and the dog leashed at first.
- [ ] Introduce one room before opening up the whole house.
- [ ] Establish a feeding, potty, and walk routine right away.
- [ ] Give plenty of decompression time and limit visitors.
- [ ] Introduce existing pets slowly and under supervision.
- [ ] Important: This is general information, not veterinary advice. Schedule a checkup and consult a veterinarian about vaccinations, parasite prevention, and your dog's health.
