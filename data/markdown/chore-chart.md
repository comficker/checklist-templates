# Chore Chart by Age

Age-appropriate chores from toddler to teen, plus a weekly rotation for the household jobs everyone shares.

> Free printable & editable version: [Chore Chart by Age on ChecklistForge](https://checklistforge.com/checklist/chore-chart) — tick items online or download as PDF.

## Ages 2-3 (toddler)

- [ ] Put toys into a basket or bin after playing — _At this age the point is the habit, not the result. Expect to do it alongside them for months._
- [ ] Carry their own plastic plate or cup to the sink
- [ ] Put dirty clothes into the laundry basket
- [ ] Fill a pet's dry food bowl using a pre-measured scoop — _An adult measures; the child pours. Keep them away from water bowls that get carried._
- [ ] Wipe up their own spills with a cloth
- [ ] Put books back on a low shelf

## Ages 4-5 (preschool)

- [ ] Make their own bed, loosely — _Pulling the duvet flat counts. Re-making it to adult standard teaches them it was never really their job._
- [ ] Set the table with napkins, cutlery and unbreakable cups
- [ ] Match clean socks into pairs
- [ ] Water indoor plants with a small jug
- [ ] Clear their own place at the table
- [ ] Sort recycling into the right bin
- [ ] Dust low surfaces and skirting boards with a dry cloth

## Ages 6-8 (early school)

- [ ] Pack their own school bag the night before — _Keep a short list taped inside the bag for the first few weeks._
- [ ] Fold and put away their own clean laundry
- [ ] Load and unload the dishwasher, skipping knives
- [ ] Wipe down the bathroom sink and mirror
- [ ] Sweep floors and use a dustpan
- [ ] Feed and water pets without reminders
- [ ] Take the bins out to the kerb on collection day
- [ ] Tidy their bedroom floor before bed

## Ages 9-11 (tween)

- [ ] Run a full load of laundry from sorting to folding — _Show them the machine once with the detergent already measured, then let them run it alone._
- [ ] Vacuum a whole room including under furniture edges
- [ ] Prepare a simple cold meal or packed lunch
- [ ] Clean the toilet with an age-safe bathroom cleaner — _Check the label and keep bleach-based products out of this age band._
- [ ] Change their own bed linen
- [ ] Rake leaves, weed a bed, or water the garden
- [ ] Help write the week's grocery list from what is missing
- [ ] Wash and dry dishes by hand

## Ages 12-18 (teen)

- [ ] Cook one family dinner a week from start to clean-up — _Fixing the night in advance stops this quietly becoming optional._
- [ ] Mow the lawn or clear snow from paths
- [ ] Do the full grocery shop from a written list
- [ ] Clean a bathroom end to end, including the floor
- [ ] Mind younger siblings for a set, agreed period
- [ ] Wash the car inside and out
- [ ] Manage their own schedule, deadlines and appointments
- [ ] Iron their own clothes
- [ ] Take responsibility for one recurring household job of their choosing — _Letting them pick it is what turns a chore into a contribution._

## Shared weekly rotation

- [ ] Kitchen deep clean: hob, microwave, fridge shelves
- [ ] Bathrooms: toilets, showers, mirrors, floors
- [ ] Floors: vacuum and mop every room
- [ ] Laundry: wash, dry, fold, put away
- [ ] Bins and recycling out
- [ ] Change all bed linen
- [ ] Grocery shop and restock staples
- [ ] Tidy shared living areas before the week starts

## Setting the chart up

- [ ] Agree the list together before anything goes on the wall — _A chart written by one person and handed down is the one that gets ignored._
- [ ] Give each person no more than three daily chores
- [ ] Fix a time of day for daily chores, such as before screens or after dinner
- [ ] Put the chart where it is seen without being fetched
- [ ] Decide in advance what happens when a chore is skipped
- [ ] Decide whether chores are paid, and keep that answer consistent
- [ ] Re-cut the list every school year as ages and schedules change
