# Estate Planning Checklist

A step-by-step estate planning checklist for US households, from writing a will to listing where every document is kept.

> Free printable & editable version: [Estate Planning Checklist on ChecklistForge](https://checklistforge.com/checklist/estate-planning-checklist) — tick items online or download as PDF.

## Take Stock

- [ ] List your assets: home, bank accounts, retirement accounts, investments, insurance, vehicles and business interests
- [ ] List your debts, such as mortgage, loans and credit cards
- [ ] Note how each asset is titled: sole name, joint, or with a beneficiary — _Joint accounts with right of survivorship and payable-on-death accounts pass directly to the named person, outside your will._
- [ ] Decide who should receive what, and who you trust to handle money and health decisions

## Will

- [ ] Write a will that names who inherits your property
- [ ] Name an executor and a backup executor
- [ ] Sign the will following your state's witness rules — _Most states require two witnesses. Many also allow a self-proving affidavit signed before a notary, which can speed up probate._
- [ ] Store the original somewhere safe that your executor can reach

## Living Trust

- [ ] Decide whether a revocable living trust fits your situation
- [ ] Name a successor trustee to take over if you die or can't manage your affairs
- [ ] Retitle assets into the trust, such as your home and bank accounts — _An unfunded trust does little. Assets left in your own name may still go through probate._
- [ ] Sign a pour-over will to catch assets left outside the trust

## Beneficiaries

- [ ] Check the primary and backup beneficiaries on every 401(k), IRA and pension
- [ ] Check the beneficiaries on every life insurance policy, including any through work
- [ ] Add payable-on-death or transfer-on-death beneficiaries to bank and brokerage accounts where offered
- [ ] Remove an ex-spouse from beneficiary forms after a divorce — _Don't assume a divorce removes them. Many employer retirement plans pay whoever is named on the form._
- [ ] Avoid naming a minor child directly; ask about a trust or custodial account instead

## If You Can't Decide for Yourself

- [ ] Sign a durable financial power of attorney — _Durable means it stays in effect if you lose capacity. Without one, your family may have to go to court to manage your money._
- [ ] Name a health care proxy, also called a health care power of attorney
- [ ] Complete an advance directive or living will that states your wishes for end-of-life care
- [ ] Sign a HIPAA authorization so your agents can talk to your doctors
- [ ] Give copies to your agents and your doctor

## Children, Dependents and Pets

- [ ] Name a guardian and a backup guardian for minor children in your will
- [ ] Decide who will manage money left to your children until they are adults
- [ ] Plan care for an adult dependent with special needs — _Leaving money directly to someone who gets SSI or Medicaid can affect their benefits. Ask an attorney about a special needs trust._
- [ ] Name a caregiver for pets and set aside money for their care

## Digital Assets and Document List

- [ ] List your online accounts, including email, banking, social media and cloud storage
- [ ] Store passwords in a password manager and plan how a trusted person can get access
- [ ] Set up legacy contacts where offered, such as on Apple, Google and Facebook accounts

## Review Triggers

- [ ] Review after marriage, divorce, a birth or an adoption
- [ ] Review after the death of a beneficiary, executor, guardian or agent
- [ ] Review after moving to another state — _Your documents usually stay valid, but power of attorney and health care forms may work more smoothly on your new state's forms._
