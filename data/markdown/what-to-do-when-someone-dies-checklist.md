# What to Do When Someone Dies Checklist

A step-by-step US checklist for the hours, weeks and months after a loved one dies, ordered by what needs to happen first.

## First 24 to 48 Hours

- [ ] Get a legal pronouncement of death — _If the person was in hospice, call the hospice team. If the death was unexpected at home, call 911. In a hospital or care facility, staff will handle it._
- [ ] Tell close family and friends, and ask one person to help spread the news
- [ ] Check for written funeral wishes or a prepaid funeral plan
- [ ] Contact a funeral home to transport the body
- [ ] Lock the home, secure valuables and car keys, and stop mail if no one lives there
- [ ] Arrange care for pets and any dependents

## Funeral and Notices

- [ ] Ask the funeral home for its itemized price list before you choose services — _Federal law requires funeral homes to give you this list, and you can buy a casket or urn elsewhere._
- [ ] Plan the funeral, burial, cremation or memorial service
- [ ] If the person served in the military, ask about VA burial benefits at VA.gov
- [ ] Write and publish an obituary
- [ ] Notify the person's employer and ask about final pay, life insurance and retirement benefits

## Death Certificates and Documents

- [ ] Order several certified copies of the death certificate — _The funeral home can usually order them for you. Many agencies accept only certified copies, not photocopies._
- [ ] Find the will, trust documents and any letter of wishes
- [ ] Gather the person's Social Security number, birth certificate, marriage certificate and military discharge papers
- [ ] Collect recent bank statements, bills and insurance policies

## Government Agencies

- [ ] Confirm the funeral home reported the death to Social Security
- [ ] Ask Social Security about survivor benefits and the one-time death payment — _Social Security doesn't pay benefits for the month of death. A payment that arrives after the death may have to be returned, so don't spend it until you check._
- [ ] Notify the VA if the person received veterans benefits
- [ ] Cancel the driver's license or state ID with the DMV
- [ ] Forward mail with the post office if needed

## Money, Accounts and Identity

- [ ] Contact each bank and credit card company the person used
- [ ] Ask Equifax, Experian and TransUnion to flag the credit report as deceased — _This helps prevent identity theft, which often targets people who have recently died. Each bureau will ask for a copy of the death certificate._
- [ ] File claims with life insurance companies and pension plans
- [ ] Cancel subscriptions, phone plans, streaming services and memberships
- [ ] Close or memorialize email and social media accounts
- [ ] Keep paying home insurance and utilities on any property until it is sold or transferred

## Will and Probate

- [ ] Give the original will to the executor
- [ ] Check with the county probate court whether probate is needed — _Many states let small estates skip full probate. The court clerk can explain your options but can't give legal advice._
- [ ] Get an employer identification number (EIN) from the IRS if the estate needs its own bank account
- [ ] Make a list of the estate's assets and debts

## Longer Term

- [ ] File the person's final federal and state income tax returns — _The final return covers January 1 to the date of death and is due on the usual tax deadline the following year. A surviving spouse can usually file jointly for that year._
- [ ] Ask a tax professional whether the estate must file its own income tax return
- [ ] Search for unclaimed money in each state where the person lived at unclaimed.org
- [ ] Update your own will and beneficiary forms if the person was named in them
- [ ] Give yourself time to grieve, and reach out for grief support if you need it
