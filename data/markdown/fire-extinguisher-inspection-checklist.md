# Fire Extinguisher Inspection Checklist

A monthly visual inspection checklist and log for portable fire extinguishers in workplaces, shops and rental buildings.

> Free printable & editable version: [Fire Extinguisher Inspection Checklist on ChecklistForge](https://checklistforge.com/checklist/fire-extinguisher-inspection-checklist) — tick items online or download as PDF.

## Location and Access

- [ ] Confirm the extinguisher is in its assigned location
- [ ] Check nothing blocks the path to it or hides it from view
- [ ] Make sure it is mounted on its bracket or in its cabinet — _Under NFPA 10, units up to 40 lb should be mounted with the top no higher than 5 ft above the floor._
- [ ] Check the cabinet opens easily and any break glass is intact

## Signage and Labels

- [ ] Check the location sign is in place where the unit is hard to see
- [ ] Make sure the operating instructions on the label face out
- [ ] Confirm the label is readable and not peeling or faded

## Pressure, Pin and Seal

- [ ] Check the pressure gauge needle is in the green zone — _CO2 units have no gauge. Check them by weight against the full weight stamped on the valve or label._
- [ ] Confirm the safety pin is in place
- [ ] Confirm the tamper seal is intact
- [ ] Lift the unit to check it feels full

## Physical Condition

- [ ] Look for dents, cracks or other damage to the cylinder
- [ ] Check for rust or corrosion on the cylinder, valve and bracket
- [ ] Check the hose for cracks, cuts or blockage
- [ ] Make sure the nozzle is clear of dirt, nests or debris
- [ ] Look for leaks or powder residue around the valve and nozzle
- [ ] Check the handle and lever are not bent or broken

## Tag and Test Dates

- [ ] Initial and date the inspection tag for this month
- [ ] Check the last annual maintenance date is within 12 months — _OSHA requires the annual maintenance date to be recorded. It is usually on the back of the tag._
- [ ] Check the hydrostatic test date on the cylinder or label — _Water, foam and CO2 units are usually due every 5 years. Most stored-pressure dry chemical units are due every 12 years._
- [ ] Note when the next 6-year maintenance is due for dry chemical units

## Right Class for the Hazard

- [ ] Confirm Class A coverage for wood, paper, cloth and trash
- [ ] Confirm Class B coverage near flammable liquids and gases
- [ ] Confirm Class C rating near energized electrical equipment — _Never use a water extinguisher on a live electrical fire._
- [ ] Use a Class D unit for combustible metals such as magnesium
- [ ] Use a Class K unit in commercial kitchens with deep fryers
