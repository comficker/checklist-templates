# Inventory Checklist

A physical inventory count checklist for small businesses, covering preparation, counting, reconciling variances and follow-up, with a sign-off section.

> Free printable & editable version: [Inventory Checklist on ChecklistForge](https://checklistforge.com/checklist/inventory-checklist) — tick items online or download as PDF.

## Plan the Count

- [ ] Set the count date, start time and expected end time
- [ ] Tell suppliers and customers that receiving and shipping will pause
- [ ] Assign a count supervisor and split staff into two-person teams — _Where you can, have staff count areas they don't usually manage. They are less likely to assume what is on the shelf._
- [ ] Print count sheets or pre-numbered count tags — _Pre-numbered tags let you check that every tag was used, voided or returned, so no area is skipped or counted twice._
- [ ] Agree on the unit of measure for each item: each, case, box or pound

## Prepare the Space

- [ ] Put away stock and move items to their correct locations
- [ ] Label or map every aisle, shelf and bin
- [ ] Separate damaged, returned and customer-owned goods and label them
- [ ] Record the last receiving and last shipping document numbers before the count — _This sets the cut-off. Anything received or shipped after these numbers belongs to the next period, not this count._
- [ ] Process all paperwork for goods received and shipped before the cut-off

## During the Count

- [ ] Have one person count and the other record
- [ ] Count one location at a time and tag it when done
- [ ] Open cases that look partly empty instead of assuming they are full
- [ ] Write clearly in pen and initial any changes rather than erasing
- [ ] Count high-value items twice, by two different people — _Compare the two counts before moving on. Fixing a mismatch on the spot is faster than a recount later._
- [ ] Check expiry dates as you count and set aside expired goods

## Reconcile the Results

- [ ] Collect all count sheets and tags and account for every tag number
- [ ] Compare counts with your inventory records
- [ ] Recount any item whose difference exceeds your set limit — _Set a limit before the count, such as a dollar amount or a percentage. That way you spend recount time on the differences that matter._
- [ ] Look for causes of variances: wrong location, wrong unit of measure or unrecorded sales or receipts

## After the Count

- [ ] Update inventory records with the final counts
- [ ] Review reorder points for items that ran low or overstocked
- [ ] Rotate stock so older items are sold or used first (FIFO) — _Put new stock behind or under older stock. This cuts waste from items that expire or go out of date._
- [ ] Write down problems found, such as theft, damage or record errors, and how to fix them
- [ ] Set a cycle counting schedule for the year ahead
