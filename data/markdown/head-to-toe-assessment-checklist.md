# Head-to-Toe Assessment Checklist

A head-to-toe physical assessment checklist for nursing students and new nurses, in the order you would perform it at the bedside.

> Free printable & editable version: [Head-to-Toe Assessment Checklist on ChecklistForge](https://checklistforge.com/checklist/head-to-toe-assessment-checklist) — tick items online or download as PDF.

## General Survey and Vital Signs

- [ ] Perform hand hygiene, introduce yourself and confirm the patient's identity with two identifiers
- [ ] Note general appearance, distress, hygiene, posture and behavior
- [ ] Measure temperature, pulse, respirations, blood pressure and oxygen saturation — _Count respirations without telling the patient, for example while you still appear to be taking the pulse. People breathe differently when they know they are being watched._
- [ ] Rate pain with a scale the patient understands, and note location, quality and onset
- [ ] Compare vital signs with the last set and the patient's baseline

## Neuro

- [ ] Assess level of consciousness: alert, lethargic, obtunded or unresponsive
- [ ] Check orientation to person, place, time and situation
- [ ] Check pupils for PERRLA: equal, round, reactive to light and accommodation
- [ ] Check hand grip and foot push strength on both sides — _Weakness on one side that is new can be a sign of stroke. Report it right away and follow your facility's stroke protocol._
- [ ] Note speech clarity and the ability to follow commands

## HEENT and Neck

- [ ] Inspect the head and face for symmetry, lesions or injury
- [ ] Inspect the eyes, ears and nose for drainage
- [ ] Inspect the mouth for moist mucous membranes, teeth or dentures and lesions
- [ ] Check that the trachea is midline and look for jugular vein distention

## Respiratory

- [ ] Inspect breathing pattern, effort, use of accessory muscles and chest shape
- [ ] Auscultate lung sounds front and back, comparing side to side — _Listen on bare skin, not through a gown. Have the patient breathe slowly through the mouth, and listen to a full breath in each spot._
- [ ] Note adventitious sounds, such as crackles, wheezes, rhonchi or stridor
- [ ] Note cough, sputum color and any oxygen device and flow rate

## Cardiovascular

- [ ] Auscultate heart sounds at the aortic, pulmonic, Erb's point, tricuspid and mitral areas
- [ ] Count the apical pulse for a full minute if the rhythm is irregular
- [ ] Palpate radial and pedal pulses on both sides and compare strength
- [ ] Check capillary refill and skin color and temperature of hands and feet
- [ ] Check for edema in the feet, ankles and legs and grade it per policy

## Abdomen and GU

- [ ] Inspect the abdomen for shape, distention, scars and wounds
- [ ] Auscultate bowel sounds in all four quadrants before palpating — _Before you chart bowel sounds as absent, follow your facility's rule for how long to listen in each quadrant, which is often several minutes._
- [ ] Lightly palpate for tenderness, firmness or masses, ending with any painful area
- [ ] Ask about last bowel movement, nausea, appetite and diet tolerance
- [ ] Note urine color, amount and any pain or trouble urinating
- [ ] If a urinary catheter is in place, check that it is secured and the bag hangs below the bladder

## Musculoskeletal and Skin

- [ ] Assess range of motion, muscle strength and gait if safe to walk
- [ ] Inspect skin color, temperature, moisture, turgor and intact areas
- [ ] Check pressure points such as the sacrum, heels and elbows for redness or breakdown — _Press on any red area. If it doesn't blanch, it may be an early pressure injury._
- [ ] Score pressure injury risk with the Braden Scale — _Braden scores range from 6 to 23. A lower score means higher risk, and 18 or below is a common cutoff for starting prevention steps._

## Safety and Documentation

- [ ] Complete a fall risk assessment with your facility's tool
- [ ] Inspect IV sites for redness, swelling, pain, leaking and the dressing date
- [ ] Check drains, tubes and wound dressings
- [ ] Make sure the bed is low and locked and the call light is within reach
- [ ] Document findings promptly and report abnormal or changed findings to the nurse or provider
